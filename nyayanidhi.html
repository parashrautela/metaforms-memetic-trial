Nyayanidhi

One legal engine, three products. A parent brand and three platform identities for legal AI.

Client Nyayanidhi ↗
Services Brand Architecture, Brand Identity, Web Design, Product UI
Year 2026

The Challenge

Nyayanidhi is building an executable state layer for law: statutes, judgments, contracts and obligations compiled into one deterministic legal state machine. It sells that engine through separate platforms to very different buyers, from enterprise legal teams to infrastructure contractors and bank recovery desks. One brand could not speak to all three, and three unrelated brands would hide the engine they share.

The Solution

A parent and three platforms. Nyayanidhi stays quiet and institutional: scales for a mark, a prism for a hero, serif type on paper. Rilin, Ferrock and Wapas each get their own name, mark and colour, and they share one skeleton underneath: the same product card, the same page structure, the same interface grammar. Each buyer sees a product built for them, and the family still reads as one company.

Phase 01

Workshop + Architecture

Ran the brand workshop with the founders, collected the moodboard and mapped the site architecture: a parent landing page, a platform for each vertical and one shared core structure underneath.

Phase 02

One Parent, Three Marks

Drew the Nyayanidhi scales, then a mark, a wordmark and a palette for each platform. Rilin in navy, Ferrock in oxblood, Wapas in forest green. Tested each one in acrylic and print.

Phase 03

Sites + Product UI

Wireframed with annotations, then designed four sites on one shared template, the interface moments that explain each product, and every section again for mobile.

One engine, many front doors

We started with the founders in a workshop: what Nyayanidhi is, who it is for and what it should never look like. The moodboard pulled from courtrooms, ledgers and case files rather than from other software. The architecture map settled the structure. One parent, a platform for each vertical, and a shared core underneath all of them.

The Parent

Nyayanidhi is the company behind the platforms, so its brand does the least talking. The mark is a set of scales drawn from vertical bars. The hero is a prism: many sources of law go in, one answer comes out. The type is a quiet serif on paper and ink, closer to a judgment than to a product page.

Rilin: Ship

Rilin is contractual liability control for enterprises. It closes the gap between what a contract says and what the business actually does after signature. The mark is five columns rising to a centre peak, set in navy and sand. The site sells four operations as working surfaces rather than a feature list, and ends on the line that sums up the product: the contracts are already signed, do you know what they say?

Ferrock: Build

Ferrock is for construction. It structures execution data into live claims while the project is still being built, not years later in a dispute. The mark is a set of interlocking beams on a construction grid, in oxblood and cream. The interface moments carry the story: a critical path that shows where a delay began, a notice clock that never lapses, and portfolio exposure in one number.

Wapas: Recover

Wapas is money recovery for institutional credit books. It runs an early warning system on a lender’s book and the full statutory recovery sequence beneath it, for banks, NBFCs and ARCs. The mark is a bar chart with a returning arrow, in forest green and lime. The interface moments show the mechanism: borrower data pulled from every source, accounts flagged by days past due, and SARFAESI notices drafted and reviewed by counsel before anyone presses send.
