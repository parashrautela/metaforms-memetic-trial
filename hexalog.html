Hexalog

The supply-chain operating layer for cross-border commerce.

Client Hexalog ↗
Services Brand Identity, Brand Guidelines, Design System, Web & Motion
Year 2026

The Challenge

Hexalog moves freight across borders for two hundred brands: forwarding, customs, warehousing, last mile. But it presented like every other logistics vendor, a service in a stack of services, judged on rate cards. It needed an identity that made it the layer everything else plugs into, not another line on the invoice.

The Solution

A brand built as infrastructure. One idea, one partner from factory to doorstep, carried through to a hexagon that reads as a container from one angle and a forward arrow from the other, a deep-purple and iris system with a signal-green accent, and a mascot that carries the warmth the data plane can’t.

Phase 01

Positioning

Ran a four-board workshop down to a single idea: Hexalog isn’t a carrier, it’s the orchestration layer between the factory floor and the customer’s door. Mapped the archetype territory to find where Hexalog actually sits.

Phase 02

Identity + System

Turned the box into a mark: a hexagon built from three isometric planes that reads as a container, a package and a forward arrow depending on how you hold it. Built the system around it: primary purple, iris, signal green, Hauora and Geist.

Phase 03

Deployment

Brand guidelines, a sixty-slide deck kit, a two-hundred-icon logistics set, fleet and container livery, office signage, a mascot system and a responsive site with an illustrated hero. Shipped across every breakpoint.

Brand Workshop

We ran a four-board workshop with the Hexalog founders covering brand essence, DNA, meta questions and vibe. The core tension surfaced early: whether Hexalog was a logistics operator that had bought software, or a technology company that happened to move boxes. We mapped what they wanted to avoid, what they wanted to achieve and what had to be retained from the existing identity. The team landed on operating layer, which set the direction for everything that followed.

four boards: essence, dna, meta questions, vibe

One partner, factory to doorstep

Cross-border freight is a relay of handoffs: origin pickup, quality check, consolidation, customs, last mile. Every handoff is a different vendor with a different system. What a brand can do is make the whole relay look like one company. That was the job.

Early Visual Direction

We audited how logistics and infrastructure brands mark themselves, from Maersk, DHL and Flexport to FedEx and Delhivery, and found a category split between arrows and globes, with almost no one owning volume. Hexalog already had a hexagon in the name. We explored it as a wordmark, as an isometric box and as a lockup that holds at 24 pixels in a browser tab and at forty feet on a container door.

From box to hexagon

The box resolved into a hexagon: three isometric planes that read as a container, a package and a forward arrow depending on how you hold it. It holds at 24 pixels in a browser tab and at forty feet on a container door, which is the only test that mattered.

Brand in space

Signage and Objects

We took the mark off screen, building signage, reception panels, desk objects and container livery. It had to read from across a warehouse floor and from six inches. Purple on white held at both, which fixed the pairing.

The network

Factory floor to doorstep

Two hundred brands, forty-plus lanes and a customs desk in every one of them. We mapped the real journey first: origin pickup, quality check, consolidation, clearance, last mile. The brand was built against the actual network, not a pitch-deck version.

Website

Structure Before Surface

We wireframed the site in grey first: hero, proof strip, problem voices, the reframe, the offer. The argument was settled before anything looked like anything. The finished site carries one claim: one partner, factory to doorstep. Shipped responsive across desktop, tablet and mobile.

The campaign

One claim, every surface

The campaign runs in logistics’ own formats: the billboard on the port road, the container door, the Earth Day post. One claim, one partner, factory to doorstep.

A brand you can carry

Totes, tees, keychains, lanyards, stickers and ID cards. Each surface tests the mark differently: drawn for embroidery, injection moulding, screen print and die-cut, from a lanyard clip to a container door. Where the full lockup got too tight, the hexagon ran on its own. For a company built on handovers, merch isn’t swag. It’s the first thing a new hire is handed.
