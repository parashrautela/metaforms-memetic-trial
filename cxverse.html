CXVERSE

Nine products, one universe. A loyalty platform rebuilt as customer experience infrastructure.

Client CXVERSE ↗
Services Brand System, Web Design & Development, Product & Dashboard UI
Year 2025–2026

The Challenge

Froogal spent eight years building the loyalty and engagement layer behind 100+ consumer brands. Now it was becoming CXVERSE, and that was a re-architecture rather than a rename. Twelve products built up under different names had to read as one suite, and a loyalty tool had to start reading as customer experience infrastructure, in a SaaS category where everything already looks like everything else.

The Solution

CXVERSE came with a logo. We designed everything that had to hold it up. One page skeleton with a colour and an emblem per product, imagery rooted in real restaurants and shop floors, a Webflow site the client owned from day one, and a dashboard direction built to say what to do next instead of only showing the numbers.

Phase 01 · Nov–Dec 2025

Brand System + Direction

Mapped the product architecture, wireframed the story and explored hero directions. Built on the colours CXVERSE already owned, with real human and retail imagery, and restructured two dashboards along the way.

Phase 02 · Jan–Feb 2026

Design Lock + Webflow Build

Design approved in the first week of January and the Webflow build started the next day. Staging was live within a month: mobile responsive, mega menu, scroll-driven sections and the blog.

Phase 03 · Mar–May 2026

Product Pages + Launch

Templatised the product pages with a colour and an emblem per module, reworked the industry pages around storytelling, locked the product icons and launched cxverse.com on 12 May 2026.

One name, twelve products

Froogal had grown into twelve separate products over eight years, each with its own name and its own logic. We started on a whiteboard: every product under one CX prefix, every industry, every resource, and a homepage that had to introduce all of it without reading like a catalogue. Wireframes came next, to settle the story before any visual work.

From loyalty tool to CX infrastructure

The hero had to move the company from a loyalty tool to customer experience infrastructure in a single screen. We tried dashboards floating over storefronts, bright daytime streets and a mall interior, then landed on a busy restaurant corner at dusk. The audience is restaurants, grocers and retailers, so the picture shows their world and not ours.

The Homepage

The homepage tells one story top to bottom. A customer's journey is scattered across the store, the app, the loyalty programme and the offers. CXVERSE pulls it into one experience. Each section takes one step of that argument: life before, the product suite, the continuity loop, the proof.

Nine Products, One Template

Every product page shares one skeleton and changes its colour. CX CRM is purple, CX Loyalty is orange, CX Pulse is green, CX Seamless is blue. Each page feels like its own product, and because the structure underneath is identical, the CXVERSE team can build the next page themselves without us.

The Product Switcher

Twelve products need a way to move between them. We designed a family of product emblems on shared geometry, then tested how the switcher should lay them out: two rows, grouped by job, or tabbed. The grid won. Every product is visible at once, with one line on what it does.

Dashboard Direction

The dashboard brief was a brand skin on shadcn. It became a rethink of what the first screen is for. A dashboard should say what is happening and what to do next, not just show the numbers. We restructured CX Seamless and CX CRM around that idea, grouped tabs that belonged together and made the terminology consistent.

Mobile

Most of CXVERSE's customers run their business from a phone, on a shop floor or behind a counter. Every section was rebuilt for small screens rather than squeezed, and the per-product colours carry across.

Brand in the World

CXVERSE arrived with its mark already drawn. Our job was everything that has to hold it up: colour, type, imagery and how the mark behaves once it leaves the screen. We tested it stitched, printed and cast in acrylic, carried it onto LinkedIn, and designed a quiet teaser campaign for the last weeks of the Froogal name.
